#### The Art of Managing One-Sided Conver
[‘But enough about you …’ How to survive in a world of monologuists, interrupters and trauma dumpers](https://www.theguardian.com/lifeandstyle/2026/sep/13/monologuists-interrupters-trauma-dumpers-survival-guide-one-sided-conversations?CMP=wellactually_email)

> There is a name for this type of egocentric behaviour – “conversational narcissism”. Although it was coined by the sociologist Charles Derber in his 1979 book The Pursuit of Attention, the term seems more relevant than ever. “WhatsApp communication and voice notes are really a series of monologues,” says Charlotte Fox Weber, a psychotherapist. “In face-to-face situations, people are forgetting how to pause naturally, to make space to hear and listen. And people are chatting on the phone – an activity that at least requires turn-taking in a defined way – much less often.”  
>   
> A lot of our communication online can feel weirdly transactional, focused on logistics or niche WhatsApp groups. According to Tamu Thomas, a life coach and author, this has damaged the art of improvised conversation. “People have less capacity for meandering conversations that don’t have a purpose,” she says. “A good dialogue is a back-and-forth between two people discovering new things about each other, even if they have known each other for years. Because there are now so many different channels available to stay in touch, some people are confusing communication with connection.”  
>   
> Knowledge is power, though. If you can recognise the five types of common conversation hogger, it will be much easier to handle them.

> Fortunately, Chaudhuri also offered tips for how to deal with some of the worst conversational offenders, including:
> 
> _**• The serial monologuist.**_ The real crime of the monologuist, says psychotherapist Charlotte Fox Weber, is not simply dominating the conversation – it is that “they often select topics that are not necessarily meaningful for anyone else.” Think: the time their baggage got lost at the airport, or their kids’ orthodontia. To handle this, try redirecting the chat without shutting the person down. Something like: “That’s really interesting, and it reminds me of….”  
> _**• The trauma dumper.**_ This is the person who overshares without listening to others, and who dominates social gatherings with long discourses about the most intimate details of his life. Chaudhuri spoke to one therapist who suggested encouraging this type of person to reach out during the good times, not only the crises.  
> _**• The boomerasker.**_ The term refers to people who ask a question simply so that they can answer it themselves. One approach is to give vague, short answers and then ask them the question back with more enthusiasm – that way you haven’t wasted time sharing. Or, you could boomerask them yourself and see how they like it.
