#### Is sexual attraction overrated
[Is sexual attraction overrated?](https://www.dazeddigital.com/beauty/article/70919/1/possible-decentre-physical-sexual-attraction-dating-appearance-sex-love)

> The stratification of individuals into different value levels or ‘[leagues](https://www.dazeddigital.com/beauty/article/70211/1/self-image-league-dating-desire-who-attracted-to-if-didnt-know-looked-like)’ within dating, based upon their perceived beauty, intelligence and career success, is a brutal reality of today’s relationship culture – one which K is actively rebelling against to assert their own value beyond looks. “As a mid- to plus-size person, depending on my weight fluctuation, it’s rare for an attractive person to also be into me. I used to hold conventionally beautiful people’s attraction to a high value and be like, ‘Oh, this really attractive person is attracted to me, so I should hold onto this relationship.’”

> With the way that [beauty culture](https://www.dazeddigital.com/beauty/article/69086/1/is-it-possible-to-divest-from-beauty-make-up-my-year-patriarchy) intertwines with white supremacy, ableism and patriarchy, as both a reflection of these values and something which can perpetuate them, it’s worth scrutinising our own preferences in partners and what they might mean. [Leanne Yau](https://www.instagram.com/polyphiliablog/?hl=en), a polyamorous relationship educator, has already witnessed some of this work being carried out within the non-monogamous community. “As a polyamorous person, something that I often deal with on the internet is people perpetuating the stereotype that polyamorous people are ugly, and it’s something that I’ve really put a lot of effort in pushing back against,” she says. 
> 
> Yau argues that gravitating towards people who don’t embody traditional beauty standards is a way of undermining the (often discriminatory) social biases about who is deserving of love. “We place a lot of emphasis, as a society, on how conventionally attractive and, therefore, how valuable someone is, to determine their overall desirability or worthiness of being in a relationship,” she explains. “I think that queer people, polyamorous people, people who have questioned relationship norms in general, are much more likely to decentre conventional attractiveness in their dating lives because we want to push against those norms.”
