#### Depression plays for the draw
[Depression plays for the draw](https://jaredhenderson.substack.com/p/depression-plays-for-the-draw) #mentalhealth #depression 

This is a surprisingly good article about depression.

> I’m sensitive to this, perhaps more than is rational. I have routinely experienced profound depression in my life, sometimes for months at a time. (Honestly, I have been in the midst of one such period for several months, though it’s getting better.) During these periods, nothing can make me happy or give me hope. I feel constantly on the brink of tears, though one feature of my depression is that it makes it nearly impossible to cry. It makes it difficult to work and difficult to read and difficult to be around my family and difficult to be alone. Even when things are objectively going well, as they are currently, I can’t feel joy. Sometimes this manifests as passive ideation, which is a polite and clinical way of saying that the idea of suicide presents itself to me. The idea of ending my own life seems to drift along in my mental haze, eventually making its way to the front of my mind, and I am confronted by the fact that this is a possible action I could take.
> 
> To be clear: I have never planned my own death, and I don’t want to commit suicide. It’s hard to write honestly about this because I worry that others will worry. If I say too little in the way of reassurance, people will worry. If I say too much, people will worry even more. All I can say is that I have spoken to people, including my wife and a mental health professional, about the fact that I’ve experienced passive ideation, and I plan to be in this world for a very long time.

> Hope is not a feeling—it’s an attitude you must adopt if you want to make any sort of difference in the world, a defiant stance against a system that would like you to give up. It’s a way of saying ‘I refuse to make no difference.’ It’s not the irrational belief that the future will be better simply because it is the future but rather the commitment that you’ll do your damnedest to make it better, whether the rest of the world is helping you or not.

> For most of the last two thousand years, party songs were censored—so almost none of them have survived. But I can make one claim with absolute assurance: While our ancestors were enjoying these feast days, they didn’t make money for huge corporations.
> 
> Life is different now. People use up their leisure time scrolling through web ‘content’ saturated with advertisements—teens especially, who devote 4-5 hours per day on these platforms. Or they binge on Netflix. Or pay to play video games.
> 
> Most of the world’s free time has now been monetized by tech companies. In fact, the monetization of leisure time is how Silicon Valley execs make their billions. Our fun is their gravy train.
> 
> As recently as a hundred years ago, leisure time existed in a media-free zone. Nobody expected you to make money for wealthy elites on your day off.
