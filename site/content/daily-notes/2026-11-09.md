#### Loneliness Epidemic and Architecture
[Can we build ourselves out of a loneliness epidemic?](https://giftarticle.ft.com/giftarticle/actions/redeem/a7461e17-bbc3-4e8e-8ef6-5717d73a7d8c)

> In 1932, Frank Lloyd Wright lamented that urban life had atomised into misery, describing the “anxious rent” that modern industrial cities such as New York forced their residents to pay. In his manifesto The Disappearing City, the US architect argued that their design reflected a historic urge to build fortresses, which stopped people from relating to one another.

> Loneliness might primarily be a social problem, but it is an architectural one too. As Lloyd Wright observed, loneliness can be caused and reinforced by the shape of our streets and the design of our housing.

> Japan could serve as a warning. In Tokyo, micro-apartments — some as small as 9 square metres — are increasingly capitalising on limited space as rents soar, up six per cent year on year, according to data from Savills. They replace the multigenerational arrangements that once kept loneliness from the door. Forty per cent of Japan’s residents reported feeling lonely this year, according to the Japanese Cabinet Office, the third highest in the world after South Korea and Turkey, according to the Human Index.
