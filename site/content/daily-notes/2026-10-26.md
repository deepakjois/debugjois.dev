#### Sleeping after Caffeine
[Biohackers Found A Way To Fall Sleep After Coffee (Maybe Forever?) \| Sprudge Coffee](https://sprudge.com/biohackers-found-a-way-to-fall-sleep-after-coffee-maybe-forever-1485380.html)

> Here’s the thing. Biohackers are tired. You would be too if you had to wake up at 3:00am to cold plunge, get in a HIIT workout, [sun your butthole](https://sprudge.com/millennial-influencers-trade-coffee-for-butthole-sunning-153671.html), and record multiple podcast episodes before the market opens and you can start day-trading AI companies. If you’re going to stay on that grind until 8:00pm you’re going to need multiple hits of caffeine throughout the day. But you also have to sleepmaxx and that means getting to bed at 10:00pm sharp, so what are you to do? You turn to rutaecarpine.
> 
> As reported by [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/biohackers-say-this-supplement-lets-you-drink-coffee-at-night-no-one-knows-if-its-safe/), many biohackers are using the supplement due to its ability to rapidly flush caffeine out of the body, thus mitigating the length of the effects. Rutaecarpine works by causing the liver to produce more of the enzyme CYP1A2, which breaks down caffeine. Thus the more CYP1A2 you have, the faster you can nullify caffeine’s effects.

#### The Importance of Friction
[Why a world without friction is a world without progress \| Aeon Essays](https://aeon.co/essays/why-a-world-without-friction-is-a-world-without-progress)

> In conversations, few words catch my attention faster than ‘friction’ – and especially talk of making things _frictionless_. From the workplace to grocery shopping, our societies seem to be striving to remove friction from everything. The motivation appears to be that decreasing resistance makes life easier and smoother.
> 
> Having spent a career as a tribologist, studying friction, wear and lubrication scientifically, I have some opinions on this. I’ll confess that, in most of my career, I too have sought to reduce friction, developing new materials, designs or even instrumentation in order to lower friction in a jet engine or reduce emissions from your car. However, I’ve also found myself trying to figure out how to increase friction to remove plaque on teeth.
> 
> The reality is that friction, despite seeming so antagonistic, is necessary and even productive. The force that resists motion has, since humans first struck a spark from a stone, been the very thing that propels us forward. And this paradox offers a lens through which to reread humanity’s entire technological history.

#### Dark Nutrition
[The Proponents of ‘Dark Nutrition’ Think Everything You Know About Food Is Wrong](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/21/magazine/dark-nutrition-raw-meat-diet.html)

> When I first started thinking about these people, I had just turned 31. I ate in a way that I considered normal and at least loosely healthful: I cooked at home and focused on vegetables and whole foods, though there were plenty of French fries and beer around the edges. But as my friends and I made our way into our 30s, I noticed people starting to pay more attention to health and wellness, adopting various diets and “protocols.” There seemed to be an infinite supply of options circulating online, some of them wildly experimental. When I described my fascination with the more outlandish ones to a friend, she forwarded me a PDF of Ray Peat’s “Nutrition for Women,” an esoteric book whose principles are echoed by some of today’s diet influencers. Ah, my friend said, _dark nutrition._
> 
> That was it: The term seemed to encompass everything from Kelly Pi’s eating to the people I’d seen promoting the health benefits of Coca-Cola. Dark nutrition wasn’t a specific diet; it was a _style_ of thinking about food and health. Its adherents had concluded that the usual sources of authority — doctors, public health officials, food executives, pharmaceutical corporations — could not be trusted. The old order that told us to eat our vegetables was discarded. New ideas emerged, via podcasts and Instagram accounts and message boards, to fill the void. These perspectives didn’t all fit together; there were arguments over low fat versus high fat, low carb versus high sugar, Silicon Valley biohacking versus strains of naturopathy that approached mysticism. Still, these realms collided and overlapped. “The raw-milk guys and homesteading girls are watching each other’s reels now,” says Sami Reiss, who [writes a popular newsletter](https://8cmbykf0.r.us-east-1.awstrack.me/L0/https:%2F%2Fsuperhealth.substack.com%2F/1/010001a122d0afff-9818aba8-497c-4cf7-853b-11c1c3a0868b-000000/iDu2AqSAssZ_ILt6jgh_ZtQS9AQ=473) about nutrition and wellness. “Whatever walls existed between those groups are disappearing.”
