#### Learning as a social activity
[Want to be a lifelong learner? Don’t go it alone \| Psyche Ideas](https://psyche.co/ideas/want-to-be-a-lifelong-learner-dont-go-it-alone)

> The internet is full of resources for self-directed learning: for any subject, you can easily find blog posts about essential texts, links to university syllabi, and hours of YouTube lectures. When I attempted to make use of these guides, however, I often found myself disengaged and unfocused. I chastised myself for scrolling through social media while watching online lectures, or forgetting most of the ideas inside the books I read.
>
> The problem, however, wasn’t my attention span. It was my approach: I was struggling because I saw learning as a solitary pursuit.

> My interest in self-directed learning eventually led me to the Brazilian philosopher Paulo Freire, one of the most influential educational theorists of the 20th century. In his impassioned manifesto _Pedagogy of the Oppressed_ (1968), he criticised the idea that ‘the teacher teaches and the students are taught … the teacher knows everything and the students know nothing.’ True learning, Freire believed, could be accomplished only ‘through the restless, impatient, continuing, hopeful inquiry human beings pursue in the world, with the world, and with each other.’

> …But stories like these helped me realise that a formal education is, for many, only the beginning of one’s intellectual and creative life. I began, slowly but surely, to piece together what my own intellectual life looked like: taking online classes with writers and literary scholars; joining in-person book clubs to deepen my understanding of challenging texts; and finding friends who offered generous, useful feedback on my work.
>
> Relationships like these might be the most important resource for learning something new. You can teach yourself almost anything, it turns out, within a community of encouraging and intellectually ambitious peers.

#### Theory-free AI

> In "theory-free AI," a programmer does not create a software model of reality and then ask a machine-learning system to use statistical insights from its training data to guess at how to operate over that model. Rather, the programmer shovels _vastly more training data_ into the AI's inbox and uses _titanic_ amounts of computing power to analyze that data and find statistical relationships _without trying to explain them_.

> The fact that a theory-free AI might demand far more cash to accomplish a task (even a "solved" one like playing chess) than an expert would charge is beside the point. Billionaires _have_ money, they _don't_ have expertise. Theory-free inference is a bid to substitute one for the other: the beauty and terror of being able to manipulate the world without studying or understanding it is that it can be done with money alone. No experts needed.
